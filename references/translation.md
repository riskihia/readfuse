# Meaning-based translation

Understand the chapter's argument, terminology and qualifications before translating its selected passages. Persist a concise source-grounded chapter-context note and glossary. Use nearby context only to understand meaning; translate only the selected coverage.

Translate all selected content in source order. Natural target-language phrasing must preserve meaning, nuance, logical relationships, uncertainty, negation, authorial voice and concrete examples. Do not condense difficult paragraphs or turn a qualified claim into certainty. Preserve headings, quotations, dialogue, numerical facts, code, equations, captions, tables and footnotes when extraction supports them; flag extraction losses.

Keep a glossary with source term, target equivalent, context and justified exceptions. Retain important terminology; use ReadFuse notes to explain it instead of replacing a precise concept with an inaccurate familiar word. Preserve proper names and protected identifiers appropriately.

Process bounded semantic units; source hashes and stable segment IDs connect outputs to originals. For oversized segments, translate ordered subparts and assemble the complete segment before recording it. Save files incrementally and never overwrite the source.

Separate ReadFuse explanations from translated prose. Do not insert examples or opinions into the author's voice. Mark genuinely unreadable passages for review; never reconstruct them from memory or outside knowledge. Inspect OCR errors in numbers, footnotes, symbols and hyphenation before trusting a translation.

## Source-to-target fidelity pass

Before drafting, identify the passage's argument and the referents of pronouns, examples, metaphors and technical terms. Keep the source visible beside the target. After drafting, account for every source sentence and meaningful clause in the target; one sentence may become several, or several may be combined, as long as no meaning is lost.

Check each passage for:

- omissions, additions, and changes to who acts, what happens, or what a pronoun refers to;
- negation, modality, possibility, certainty, quantity, scope and attribution;
- cause, contrast, condition, sequence and other logical relationships;
- concrete properties, direction, location, position and order in examples;
- references, quotations, footnote markers, ellipses, numbers and meaningful formatting.

Do not quietly soften a strong author claim, make a tentative claim certain, correct the author's argument, or promote an explanatory gloss into the translation. Put necessary context in a separately labeled ReadFuse note. If a source reference is unavailable, preserve its marker and state the limitation; do not infer a footnote marker from an ellipsis. Use surrounding chapter context to resolve ambiguity, but do not assert a detail (such as audience level or a physical object's property) unless the source supports it.

Choose natural equivalents in context. Preserve an idiom or metaphor's function and emphasis without forcing literal word order. Similar target phrases can both be valid; do not impose one vocabulary choice merely because it appeared in another example. Distinguish a substantive meaning error from a stylistic preference.

After polishing fluency, compare the complete target against the source a second time, sentence by sentence and clause by clause. Ensure list or paragraph restructuring did not drop an introductory sentence. Recheck notes affected by translation edits; keep summaries grounded in the original. Word-count ratios are warnings, not proof of fidelity. Only record a complete segment after this review.
