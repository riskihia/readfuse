# Source-grounded chapter summaries

Summarize from the original selected passages after chapter translation/notes, never solely from the translation or outside knowledge. Use source anchors in internal artifacts, without cluttering the reading edition with processing IDs.

At the end of each chapter, provide a short paragraph conveying the core discussion followed by several important points. Length and point count follow the source; do not force generic sections or quotas. Preserve central relationships, context, qualifications and distinctions. An author's proposal or opinion stays attributed, not promoted to established fact.

For partially processed chapters, summarize only the covered original segments and label the result as partial. Do not add unselected later material or pretend a page-range summary represents the full chapter. Regenerate the summary when selected coverage grows, using all currently covered original passages.

A summary descriptor lists exact selected chapter segment IDs in order. Do not store chapter summaries as independent summaries for each chunk. Do not invent titles, quotations, page labels or support for a claim. Additional teaching examples belong in labeled ReadFuse notes, not in the author's summarized argument.

Before recording, compare each summary statement with the original selected source, including qualifications and relationships. Recheck the summary if coverage changes. Translation polishing alone does not justify changing a source-grounded summary; change it only when it misstates or omits a central point.
